The short version

Three things are wrong, and none of them are about your writing, your servers being "too small", or needing a new website.

1. Three advertising banners weigh 18 MB

Three animated GIF adverts on the homepage total 18.3 MB — that is 86% of your entire page. One banner alone is 6.8 MB. On a typical Bangladeshi mobile data plan, a single visit to your homepage can cost a reader real money.

2. Your main photo is told to wait

The biggest photo on the page is marked "load this later" — a setting meant for pictures further down the page. So the browser queues it behind everything else, including those 18 MB of adverts. 72% of the 23.7-second wait is the photo simply sitting in a queue.

3. The page is assembled from 288 separate pieces

Every visit fetches 74 style files and 52 scripts. The browser must stop and wait for the style files before it can draw anything, and the advertising and tracking scripts then freeze the page for nearly 4 seconds of tapping and scrolling that goes nowhere.

What it costs you

Speed is not a vanity metric for a news site. It maps directly onto money:

  • Readers leave before the page loads. Google's own research puts bounce risk up sharply past three seconds. You are at twenty-three.
  • Adverts that never get seen are not paid for. Slow pages mean fewer viewable impressions, which lowers what advertisers will pay per thousand.
  • Google ranks slow mobile pages lower. Core Web Vitals are a live ranking signal, and the page fails all the loading ones.
  • Data cost is a real barrier. 21 MB a visit prices out exactly the mobile-first audience a national daily needs to grow.

The good news: we proved the fix

Rather than just hand over a list, we rebuilt the "Top Stories" section of your homepage — same nine articles, same photographs, same layout, same advert slot — using the techniques we're recommending. Then we measured both versions under identical conditions.

What we'd do first

We ordered every fix by how much a reader would feel it against how much work it takes. Three of them are a few hours of work and account for almost all of the gain:

FixEffortWhat the reader gains
Convert the three GIF adverts to video files2–3 hours18 MB lighter, ~15 s faster
Stop telling the top photo to load last1 hour~12–17 s faster
Delay advertising & tracking scripts until after the page draws1 dayPage stops freezing

We are also explicit about what we would not spend your budget on — four commonly-recommended "fixes" here are worth less than a second between them. That reasoning is on the fix list.

Recommended next step

Fixes 1 and 2 are configuration changes, not a rebuild. A single developer can ship both inside a day, and readers would notice the same afternoon. We suggest doing exactly that, measuring for a week, and then deciding on the advertising-script work with real numbers in hand.
