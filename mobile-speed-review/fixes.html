Part B · Prioritisation

What to fix, in what order — and what to leave alone

Ranked by reader-felt impact divided by implementation cost. Estimated savings come from the Lighthouse audit that identified each issue; where we measured the fix ourselves, it is marked measured.

Do first — 1.5 days of work, ~90% of the available gain

#FixPriorityCostEstimated savingWhy it ranks here
1 Re-encode the three GIF ad creatives as H.264/WebM video with a static poster; keep the same slot dimensions. Critical 2–3 hrs −18.2 MB
(measured: 6,780 KB → 40 KB)		 86% of page weight in three files. No code architecture changes, no ad-stack changes, no negotiation — just a different export from the creative team. Highest ratio on the page by an order of magnitude.
2 Remove loading="lazy" from the lead story image and add fetchpriority="high" plus a preload hint. Apply lazy-loading only below the fold. Critical 1 hr (one Twig template) −12–17 s LCP 72% of a 23.7 s LCP is pure queue delay caused by one attribute. Cheapest fix on the list with the largest single-metric effect. Must land with #1 — priority on an image still stuck behind 18 MB helps less.
3 Enable Drupal CSS/JS aggregation (Performance settings) and stop shipping Claro admin CSS to anonymous readers. Critical 1 hr + regression check −6–8.7 s FCP Two checkboxes in an admin screen collapse 74 blocking requests into a handful. Lowest-risk, highest-return configuration change available. The only reason it is not #1 is that it needs a visual regression pass across templates.
4 Defer the ad and tag stack: load GPT and GTM after first paint, consolidate the three gtag containers into one, drop the Facebook pixel into GTM's deferred trigger. Critical 1 day −2.5–3.5 s TBT
(measured: 3,449 ms → 0 ms)		 Turns a page that ignores taps for 3.9 s into one that responds immediately. Costs a day because it needs commercial sign-off on viewability measurement, which is the real constraint, not the code.

Stop here and you have most of it. Fixes 1–4 are the ones we reproduced in the demo, and they moved a section from 45/100 to 100/100. Everything below is worthwhile but materially smaller.

Do next — the following sprint

#FixPriorityCostEstimated savingWhy it ranks here
5 Full-page caching at the edge (CDN or Varnish) for anonymous traffic. High 2–3 days −1.8–2.5 s TTFB Improves every metric and every page, not just the homepage, and protects you on breaking-news traffic spikes. Ranked below the top four purely on cost and infrastructure risk.
6 AVIF/WebP derivatives for Drupal image styles, with PNG photographs switched to lossy formats. High 1 day −600–700 KB
(measured: 1,720 KB → 222 KB across 9 images)		 Real, permanent, applies to every article page. Below #1 only because editorial images are already a small fraction of the weight compared with the GIF ads.
7 Cache-Control headers: one-year immutable TTLs on fingerprinted static assets. High 2–3 hrs −790 KB on repeat visits Cheap, and news audiences are daily returners so the compounding value is higher here than the raw number suggests. Invisible on a Lighthouse cold-load score, which is exactly why it gets forgotten.
8 Add preconnect for securepubads.g.doubleclick.net, googletagmanager.com and the font origin. Medium 15 min −340 ms Four lines of HTML. Trivial, so worth doing alongside #2 even though the absolute saving is modest — the only reason it is not top-four is that it cannot help while the main thread is jammed.
9 Self-host fonts with font-display: swap and subsetting. Medium 4 hrs −150–300 ms, steadier text render Small but removes a third-party dependency from the critical path.
10 Audit unused JavaScript (542 KB shipped, never executed) and remove redundant tracking (Clarity + Chartbeat + GA4 ×3 overlap heavily). Medium 2–3 days (mostly stakeholder work) −300–500 ms Genuinely valuable but slow: every tag has an internal owner who will defend it. Start the conversation now, expect it to land after the quick wins.

Deliberately not doing

Being explicit about what to skip is as much a part of the job as the fix list. These all appear in the audit and we would decline all of them.

Audit itemNominal savingVerdictReasoning
Minify JavaScript 6 KB Skip 6 KB on a 21,424 KB page is 0.03%. It is a rounding error against a single GIF frame, and touching the build to get it risks more than it returns.
Remove legacy JavaScript polyfills 13 KB Skip Same reasoning, plus most of the polyfilled code sits inside third-party bundles you do not control. Cost is high, control is low, payoff is negligible.
Reduce DOM size (1,984 elements) Unquantified Skip Under 3,000 elements is unremarkable for a news homepage, and it is not what is causing the 3.9 s of blocking — the tag scripts are. Reducing it means re-architecting the front page for a metric no reader feels.
Fix Cumulative Layout Shift CLS 0.021 Skip Already passing comfortably (threshold 0.1). Spending here would be optimising something that is not broken. Just don't regress it: keep width/height on every image and reserve the ad slot with aspect-ratio.
"Rebuild the site on a modern framework" — Skip The most expensive answer and the wrong one. Nothing in this diagnosis is caused by Drupal being Drupal — it is caused by unaggregated assets, an attribute on one image tag, and GIF ad creative. Our demo hits 100/100 with plain HTML, but a Drupal page with fixes 1–4 gets substantially the same reader experience for roughly 1.5 days of work instead of a six-figure replatform.
